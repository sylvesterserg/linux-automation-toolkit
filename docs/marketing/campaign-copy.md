# Social campaign copy

## Voice rules

* Sound calm, capable, and practical.
* Use short sentences and plain language.
* Respect the owner's accomplishment. Never shame people for using AI tools.
* Lead with a symptom the owner recognizes, then explain the next step.
* Avoid fear based claims and guaranteed outcomes.
* Use at most one CTA per post.

## Week 1: recognize the moment

### Wednesday: ownership question

Your app is live. But can you answer these three questions?

1. Where is the customer data stored?
2. Who controls the domain, code, and hosting accounts?
3. What happens if your builder changes its pricing or terms?

Fast building is a strength. Clear ownership is what protects that progress.

### Friday: conversation post

Small business owners who built an app with AI: what is the one part you are
least confident about?

Security, reliability, integrations, cost, or something else?

Reply with one word. I am using the answers to create practical guides.

## Week 2: teach the five checks

### Monday: infographic

Before you send more traffic to your AI built app, check five things:

1. Secrets are not visible in the browser.
2. Forms and logins have bot and rate limit protection.
3. Errors are logged and someone receives an alert.
4. Backups exist and a restore has been tested.
5. You can export your code and customer data.

Not sure where to look? Comment **REVIEW** and I will send the checklist.

### Wednesday: bot protection

If your public form accepts unlimited submissions, it is not only collecting
leads. It may be collecting spam, wasting database space, and triggering email
costs.

Bot protection, rate limits, and a hidden honeypot can reduce the noise without
making real customers solve a puzzle.

### Friday: production lesson

“It worked in testing” can be completely true.

Production adds real browsers, slow connections, repeated clicks, unexpected
inputs, and traffic spikes. That is why a launch review looks beyond the happy
path.

What happened the first time a real customer used your app?

## Week 3: demonstrate the work

### Monday: mini audit format

The first 30 minutes of an app review are not about rewriting code.

I map the basics:

* What can the public reach?
* What data enters and leaves?
* Where are keys and secrets stored?
* What fails silently?
* Which repeated task should be automated?

Clarity comes before changes.

### Wednesday: automation value

A new lead fills out your app's form.

Does the information automatically reach the right inbox or CRM? Does the lead
receive a useful confirmation? Does someone get an alert if delivery fails?

If not, the app may be working while the business process around it is not.

### Friday: authority without overclaiming

Three common findings in early stage apps:

* A secret placed in frontend code
* No useful alert when a key workflow fails
* A manual process that could be one reliable automation

These are fixable. Finding them before growth makes the fix less disruptive.

## Short video scripts

### Video 1: the 30 second security check

**Hook:** “If you built your business app with AI, check this before you share
the link again.”

**Body:** “Open your browser's developer tools and look at the network requests.
You should not see private API keys or admin credentials. Public identifiers
are normal. Private secrets are not. If you cannot tell the difference, pause
and have the app reviewed before adding more customer data.”

**Close:** “Follow Sylvect for practical ways to make a fast build more solid.”

### Video 2: the silent failure

**Hook:** “The most expensive app error may be the one nobody sees.”

**Body:** “A customer submits a lead form. The page says success, but the email
or CRM step fails. Unless the workflow logs the error and alerts someone, that
lead disappears quietly. Test the full business process, not only the button.”

**Close:** “Comment REVIEW if you want the five point readiness checklist.”

### Video 3: no automatic rebuild

**Hook:** “No, your AI built app does not automatically need a rebuild.”

**Body:** “Start by identifying the real gaps. Access controls, bot protection,
monitoring, backups, and a few weak workflows can often be improved without
throwing away the whole product.”

**Close:** “Review first. Prioritize second. Change only what earns its place.”

## Measurement

Track the funnel rather than likes alone:

| Metric | What it reveals |
| --- | --- |
| Saves and shares | The topic is useful and specific |
| Qualified profile visits | The hook reached the intended audience |
| Landing page visits by UTM | The channel created intent |
| Form completion rate | The offer and form are clear |
| Booked and attended calls | Follow up is working |
| Review to implementation rate | Findings lead to valuable work |

After four weeks, reuse the two posts that created the most qualified landing
page visits. Rewrite weak hooks before changing the offer.
